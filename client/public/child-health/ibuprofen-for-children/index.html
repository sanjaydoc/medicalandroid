Ibuprofen for children

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Ibuprofen relieves pain and fever and reduces inflammation, but it is harder on the stomach and kidneys than paracetamol and is not suitable for every child. Here is how it is used safely.

Key points on children’s ibuprofen

  • Usual dose is about 5–10 mg per kg per dose, every 6–8 hours
  • Always give with or after food to protect the stomach
  • Dose by weight and match the syrup strength
  • Not for babies under 3 months
  • Keep the child well hydrated while using it

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Avoid in dehydration, chickenpox, or a child who is not drinking (kidney risk)
  • Avoid with a history of stomach ulcers, asthma flares from NSAIDs, or kidney problems
  • Tummy pain, black stools or vomiting blood — stop and seek care
  • A very unwell child needs a doctor, not just medicine
General reference — confirm the exact dose. The exact dose depends on your child’s weight and the product strength — follow the pack or your doctor/pharmacist. Paracetamol is often preferred when a child is dehydrated. MedDroid cannot prescribe.

Frequently asked questions

How much ibuprofen for a child?

Around 5–10 mg per kg per dose every 6–8 hours, with food, measured to the syrup strength. Confirm the exact amount with your pharmacist.

When should I avoid ibuprofen in my child?

Avoid it if they are dehydrated, not drinking, have chickenpox, or have kidney/stomach problems — paracetamol is safer in those cases.

Paracetamol or ibuprofen for a child?

Either can help fever and pain; ibuprofen also reduces inflammation but needs food and caution in dehydration. Ask a pharmacist which suits your child.
