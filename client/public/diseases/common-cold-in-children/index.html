Common Cold in Children

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Colds are the most frequent illness of childhood, and young children can catch several a year. They are caused by viruses and almost always clear on their own.

Causes & risk factors

  • Common cold viruses (many different types)
  • Spread by coughs, sneezes and touching surfaces
  • Close contact at creche, school or home
  • More frequent in the cooler months

Common symptoms

  • Runny or blocked nose
  • Sore throat
  • Cough
  • Mild fever
  • Sneezing
  • Being a bit off feeds or fussy

Tests & diagnosis

  • Usually none — diagnosed by symptoms
  • CBC (only if unusually unwell)

Treatment & management

  • Plenty of fluids and rest
  • Saline nose drops and gentle suction for a blocked nose in babies
  • Keep the child comfortable; fever medicine for discomfort at a weight-based dose confirmed by a doctor
  • Avoid cough and cold medicines in young children unless a doctor advises
  • Encourage handwashing to reduce spread

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Difficulty breathing, fast breathing or ribs pulling in — seek urgent care
  • A baby under 3 months with a fever — urgent
  • Not drinking, very drowsy or worsening after a few days
  • A cough lasting more than 2-3 weeks

Frequently asked questions

Do children need antibiotics for a cold?

No. Colds are caused by viruses, which antibiotics do not treat. Antibiotics are only needed if a bacterial complication develops, and that is for a doctor to decide.

How many colds a year is normal?

Young children, especially at creche or school, can have six to eight or more colds a year. This is normal as their immune system meets new viruses.
