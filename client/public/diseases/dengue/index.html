Dengue Fever

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection common in India, especially in the monsoon. Most people recover, but some develop a dangerous phase with bleeding or shock that needs urgent care.

Causes & risk factors

  • Dengue virus spread by the Aedes mosquito
  • Bites usually during the day
  • More common in and after the rainy season
  • Standing water where mosquitoes breed

Common symptoms

  • High fever
  • Severe headache and pain behind the eyes
  • Severe joint and muscle pain
  • Skin rash
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Easy bruising or bleeding gums

Tests & diagnosis

  • Dengue test (NS1/antibodies)
  • CBC
  • Platelet count
  • Haematocrit (PCV)
  • Liver function (LFT)

Treatment & management

  • Rest and plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration
  • Paracetamol for fever (avoid aspirin and ibuprofen)
  • Close monitoring of platelet count and warning signs
  • Hospital care with IV fluids in severe cases
  • Watching especially during the critical phase as fever falls

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Severe abdominal pain or persistent vomiting — urgent
  • Bleeding gums, nose or in vomit/stool
  • Drowsiness, restlessness or cold clammy skin
  • Reduced urine or fainting

Frequently asked questions

When is dengue most dangerous?

The critical phase often begins as the fever drops (around days 3 to 7). Warning signs like severe abdominal pain, vomiting or bleeding need immediate hospital care.

Which painkillers should I avoid in dengue?

Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen, which increase bleeding risk. Paracetamol is the safe choice for fever and pain — but confirm with your doctor.
