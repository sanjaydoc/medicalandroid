Diarrhoea & Dehydration in Children

Diarrhoea is common in children and usually caused by a passing infection. The main risk is dehydration, which oral rehydration solution (ORS) prevents effectively.

Causes & risk factors

  • Viral gut infections (rotavirus and others)
  • Bacterial infections from contaminated food or water
  • Parasites
  • Food intolerance or a reaction to some medicines
  • Poor sanitation and unclean water in some settings

Common symptoms

  • Loose, watery stools
  • Vomiting
  • Tummy cramps
  • Fever
  • Dry mouth, few tears, sunken eyes (dehydration)
  • Passing little urine

Tests & diagnosis

  • Usually none — assessed clinically
  • Stool test (if blood, mucus or persistent)
  • Electrolytes (if severely dehydrated)

Treatment & management

  • Give ORS (oral rehydration solution) frequently in small sips
  • Continue breastfeeding or normal feeds as tolerated
  • Zinc supplements for a course as recommended for children
  • Avoid sugary fizzy drinks
  • Wash hands well and use clean water and food to prevent spread

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Signs of dehydration: very drowsy, sunken eyes, no tears, little urine — seek urgent care
  • Blood in the stool, or persistent vomiting so fluids cannot be kept down — urgent
  • A baby under 6 months with diarrhoea — get seen promptly
  • Diarrhoea lasting more than a few days or a high fever

Frequently asked questions

What is the best treatment for a child’s diarrhoea?

ORS to prevent dehydration, plus continued feeding and zinc, is the mainstay. Most infections clear on their own, so the focus is on keeping the child well hydrated.

Should I stop feeding during diarrhoea?

No. Continue breastfeeding and normal food as tolerated. Stopping feeds slows recovery. Just add ORS to replace the extra fluid being lost.
