Ectopic Pregnancy

An ectopic pregnancy is when a fertilised egg implants outside the womb, usually in a fallopian tube. It cannot survive and is a medical emergency, so early signs must not be ignored.

Causes & risk factors

  • Previous pelvic infection or PID scarring the tubes
  • Previous ectopic pregnancy or tubal surgery
  • Endometriosis
  • Pregnancy with an IUD in place
  • Assisted conception (IVF) slightly raises the risk
  • Often no clear cause is found

Common symptoms

  • One-sided lower tummy pain
  • Vaginal bleeding or spotting in early pregnancy
  • Shoulder-tip pain
  • Dizziness
  • Fainting or collapse
  • Pain when passing urine or stool

Tests & diagnosis

  • Pregnancy hormone (beta-hCG)
  • Transvaginal ultrasound
  • Complete blood count (CBC)
  • Blood group and Rh typing

Treatment & management

  • This is an emergency — treatment is arranged in hospital
  • Medicine to end an early, stable ectopic in selected cases
  • Keyhole surgery to remove the pregnancy, sometimes with the tube
  • Urgent surgery if there is heavy internal bleeding
  • Emotional support and follow-up afterwards

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Severe one-sided tummy pain in early pregnancy — seek urgent care immediately
  • Feeling faint, dizzy or collapsing — call for emergency help
  • Shoulder-tip pain with a positive pregnancy test — urgent
  • Any bleeding with pain in early pregnancy — get seen urgently

Frequently asked questions

Why is an ectopic pregnancy dangerous?

The pregnancy cannot grow safely outside the womb, and as it enlarges it can rupture the tube and cause life-threatening internal bleeding. That is why early symptoms need urgent care.

Can I have a normal pregnancy after an ectopic?

Yes, most women go on to have healthy pregnancies afterwards, though the risk of another ectopic is slightly higher. Early scans in the next pregnancy help confirm it is in the right place.
