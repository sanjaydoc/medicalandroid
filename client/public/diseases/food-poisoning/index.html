Food Poisoning

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Food poisoning is illness from eating food or water contaminated with bacteria, viruses or their toxins. It usually causes vomiting and diarrhoea that settle within a few days.

Causes & risk factors

  • Bacteria such as Salmonella, E. coli or Campylobacter
  • Viruses such as norovirus and rotavirus
  • Toxins in food left out too long
  • Contaminated or untreated water
  • Poor hand and kitchen hygiene

Common symptoms

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Diarrhoea, sometimes with blood
  • Tummy cramps
  • Fever
  • Loss of appetite
  • Dizziness from dehydration

Tests & diagnosis

  • Usually none — diagnosis is clinical
  • Stool test if symptoms are severe or prolonged
  • CBC (if unwell)
  • Electrolytes (if dehydrated)

Treatment & management

  • Rest and drink plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration
  • Oral rehydration salts (ORS) to replace lost salts and water
  • Eat light, plain food once vomiting settles
  • Avoid anti-diarrhoeal medicines if there is blood or high fever
  • Antibiotics only for specific bacterial infections, on medical advice

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Signs of dehydration — very little urine, sunken eyes, extreme thirst
  • Blood in vomit or stool
  • High fever or diarrhoea lasting more than a few days
  • Symptoms in a baby, elderly or pregnant person, or someone very unwell

Frequently asked questions

How long does food poisoning last?

Most cases settle within 1-3 days. See a doctor if you cannot keep fluids down, become dehydrated, or have blood in your stool or a high fever.

Should I take antibiotics for food poisoning?

Usually not — most cases are viral or self-limiting. Antibiotics are only for certain bacterial infections and must be prescribed. Rehydration is the main treatment.
