Gynaecomastia (Male Breast Enlargement)

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Gynaecomastia is enlargement of breast tissue in men, caused by an imbalance between male and female hormones. It is common at puberty and in older age and is usually harmless.

Causes & risk factors

  • Hormone changes at puberty (often settles on its own)
  • Ageing, especially with weight gain
  • Certain medicines and some recreational drugs
  • Liver, kidney or thyroid disease
  • Low testosterone
  • Rarely, hormone-producing tumours

Common symptoms

  • Swelling or firmness under one or both nipples
  • Breast tenderness
  • A rubbery disc of tissue behind the nipple
  • Occasionally nipple discomfort

Tests & diagnosis

  • Testosterone
  • Liver function (LFT)
  • Thyroid function (TFT)
  • Oestrogen and hCG if needed
  • Breast ultrasound if a lump is firm

Treatment & management

  • Reassurance and observation, as many cases resolve
  • Stopping or changing a responsible medicine
  • Treating an underlying cause (thyroid, liver, hormone problem)
  • Weight loss where relevant
  • Medicines or surgery for persistent, distressing cases

When to see a doctor urgently

  • A firm, hard or fixed lump, or one on one side only
  • Nipple discharge or skin changes
  • Rapidly growing or painful breast tissue
  • Breast enlargement with other hormone symptoms

Frequently asked questions

Is gynaecomastia the same as chest fat?

No. Gynaecomastia is true breast (glandular) tissue, felt as a firm disc behind the nipple, whereas fatty chest tissue is soft and diffuse. An examination tells them apart.

Can it be a sign of cancer?

Male breast cancer is rare but possible, especially with a hard, one-sided lump, skin changes or discharge. Such features should be checked promptly.
