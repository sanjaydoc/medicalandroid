Kidney Stones

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Kidney stones are hard deposits that form in the kidneys and can cause severe pain as they move down the urinary tract. They are common in hot climates and with low fluid intake.

Causes & risk factors

  • Not drinking enough fluids
  • Diet high in salt or oxalate-rich foods
  • High uric acid (linked to certain diets)
  • Recurrent urine infections
  • Family history or certain metabolic conditions

Common symptoms

  • Severe back or side (loin) pain
  • Pain spreading to the groin
  • Blood in urine
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Painful urination
  • Frequent urge to urinate

Tests & diagnosis

  • Urine test
  • Ultrasound (KUB)
  • CT scan (non-contrast KUB)
  • Creatinine
  • Uric acid
  • Calcium

Treatment & management

  • Plenty of fluids to help small stones pass
  • Pain relief and anti-sickness medicines
  • Medicines to help a stone pass in some cases
  • Shockwave therapy (lithotripsy) or keyhole procedures for larger stones
  • Preventive advice — more fluids, less salt, dietary changes

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Severe pain with fever or chills — urgent (possible infected, blocked kidney)
  • Vomiting so bad you cannot keep fluids down
  • Blood in the urine with severe pain
  • Being unable to pass urine

Frequently asked questions

How can I prevent kidney stones?

Drink plenty of water throughout the day, cut down on salt, and follow any diet advice for your stone type. Staying well hydrated is the single most effective step.

Do all kidney stones need surgery?

No. Many small stones pass on their own with fluids and pain relief. Larger stones, or those causing blockage or infection, may need lithotripsy or a procedure.
