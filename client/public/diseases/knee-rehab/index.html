Knee Rehabilitation

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Knee rehabilitation strengthens the muscles around the knee and restores movement after injury, surgery or arthritis. It reduces pain and improves stability for daily activities.

Causes & risk factors

  • Recovery after ligament or meniscus injury
  • After knee replacement or surgery
  • Osteoarthritis of the knee
  • Muscle weakness or instability
  • Overuse or sports strain

Common symptoms

  • Knee pain
  • Stiffness and reduced movement
  • Weakness or knee giving way
  • Swelling
  • Difficulty climbing stairs or squatting

Tests & diagnosis

  • Clinical and functional assessment
  • Range-of-movement and strength testing
  • X-ray
  • MRI

Treatment & management

  • Strengthening exercises for the thigh and hip muscles
  • Range-of-movement and stretching exercises
  • Balance and stability training
  • Pain and swelling management
  • Guidance on stairs, squatting and daily tasks
  • Gradual return to activity

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Knee that repeatedly gives way or locks
  • Severe swelling or inability to bear weight
  • A hot, red, very swollen knee (possible infection)
  • No improvement with rehabilitation

Frequently asked questions

Why strengthen the thigh muscles for knee pain?

Strong thigh (quadriceps) and hip muscles support and stabilise the knee, reducing load on the joint and easing pain.

Is squatting bad for the knees?

Deep, heavy squatting can strain painful knees, but guided, controlled exercises are usually beneficial. A physiotherapist will advise safe limits.
