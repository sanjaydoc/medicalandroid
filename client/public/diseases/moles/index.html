Moles

Moles are common skin growths made of pigment cells. Most are harmless, but changes in a mole can occasionally signal skin cancer, so monitoring is important.

Causes & risk factors

  • Clusters of pigment-making cells
  • Genetic factors
  • Sun exposure
  • Hormonal changes (such as pregnancy)
  • Appearing from childhood through adulthood

Common symptoms

  • Brown, black or skin-coloured spots
  • Round or oval shape with smooth edges
  • Flat or slightly raised
  • Usually stable in size and colour
  • Changes are the main warning sign

Tests & diagnosis

  • Clinical skin examination
  • Dermoscopy
  • Skin biopsy if a mole looks suspicious

Treatment & management

  • Most moles need no treatment
  • Monitoring for changes over time
  • Removal of suspicious moles for testing
  • Removal for cosmetic reasons or if irritated
  • Sun protection to limit new moles

When to see a doctor urgently

  • A mole that changes in size, shape or colour
  • A mole that bleeds, itches or crusts
  • A new mole appearing in adulthood
  • A mole with irregular edges or uneven colour

Frequently asked questions

When is a mole a cause for concern?

Watch for changes in size, shape, colour or border, and for bleeding or itching. Any such change should be checked by a doctor.

Is it safe to remove a mole for cosmetic reasons?

Yes, moles can be removed by a doctor. It is best not to attempt removal yourself, as this risks infection and misses the chance to test the mole.
