Obesity

Obesity is carrying excess body fat that harms health, usually judged by body mass index (BMI). It raises the risk of diabetes, heart disease and many other conditions.

Causes & risk factors

  • Taking in more energy than the body uses over time
  • A diet high in sugar, refined carbs and fatty foods
  • Low physical activity and long sitting hours
  • Genetic and hormonal factors
  • Some medicines and conditions (e.g. underactive thyroid)
  • Poor sleep and stress

Common symptoms

  • Excess weight and a high BMI or waist size
  • Breathlessness on exertion
  • Joint and back pain
  • Tiredness and low energy
  • Snoring or disturbed sleep
  • Low mood or reduced confidence

Tests & diagnosis

  • Blood sugar and HbA1c
  • Lipid profile
  • Thyroid test
  • LFT (for fatty liver)
  • BMI and waist measurement

Treatment & management

  • A balanced, calorie-controlled diet with dietitian support
  • Regular physical activity built up gradually
  • Behaviour and sleep support
  • Weight-loss medicines in selected people, under medical care
  • Bariatric (weight-loss) surgery for severe obesity when other steps have not worked

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Difficulty losing weight despite diet and exercise
  • Weight linked to diabetes, high BP or sleep apnoea
  • Breathlessness, chest pain or severe joint problems
  • Interest in medical or surgical weight-loss options

Frequently asked questions

What BMI counts as obesity?

A BMI of 30 or above is generally called obesity, and 25-30 is overweight. For South Asians, lower cut-offs (from about 23 and 27.5) are used, as health risks start at a lower BMI.

Is obesity just about willpower?

No. Genetics, hormones, sleep, stress, medicines and environment all play a part. Effective treatment combines diet, activity and, when needed, medical or surgical help.
