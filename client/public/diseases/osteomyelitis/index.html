Bone Infection (Osteomyelitis)

Osteomyelitis is an infection of the bone, usually caused by bacteria. It can develop from a spreading infection, an open wound or surgery, and needs prompt treatment.

Causes & risk factors

  • Bacteria spreading through the blood
  • An open fracture or deep wound
  • Infection after bone surgery
  • Diabetic foot ulcers
  • Weakened immunity

Common symptoms

  • Fever and chills
  • Deep bone pain
  • Swelling, warmth and redness over the bone
  • Tiredness and feeling unwell
  • Pus draining from a wound

Tests & diagnosis

  • CBC
  • CRP
  • ESR
  • MRI
  • X-ray
  • Bone biopsy and culture

Treatment & management

  • A course of appropriate antibiotics as prescribed
  • Surgery to drain pus or remove dead bone in some cases
  • Treating the source, such as a diabetic foot ulcer
  • Rest and immobilisation of the affected part
  • Close monitoring with blood tests

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Fever with severe or worsening bone pain
  • Redness, swelling or pus over a bone or wound
  • A non-healing wound in a person with diabetes
  • Feeling very unwell with a known bone injury

Frequently asked questions

Is osteomyelitis dangerous?

It can be, if untreated. Early antibiotics and, when needed, surgery usually control the infection and prevent lasting bone damage.

Why are diabetics at higher risk?

Poor circulation and nerve damage in diabetes lead to foot wounds that heal slowly, allowing infection to reach the bone.
