Pharyngitis (Sore Throat)

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Pharyngitis is inflammation of the pharynx, the back of the throat. It causes a scratchy, painful throat and is most often due to a viral infection.

Causes & risk factors

  • Viral infections such as the common cold or flu
  • Bacterial infection such as streptococcus
  • Dry air, smoke or air pollution
  • Acid reflux
  • Overuse of the voice
  • Allergies with post-nasal drip

Common symptoms

  • Sore, scratchy throat
  • Painful swallowing
  • Fever
  • Cough
  • Hoarse voice
  • Swollen neck glands

Tests & diagnosis

  • Throat examination
  • Throat swab for strep
  • Complete blood count (CBC)

Treatment & management

  • Rest, warm fluids and salt-water gargles
  • Lozenges and pain relief for comfort
  • Staying hydrated
  • Antibiotics only when a bacterial cause is confirmed
  • Avoiding smoke and irritants

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  • Sore throat lasting more than a week
  • High fever, rash or drooling
  • A lump in the neck or persistent hoarseness

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if it is strep throat?

Strep tends to cause a sudden severe sore throat with fever and no cough. A throat swab is the only way to confirm it and decide if antibiotics are needed.

How long does a sore throat last?

Most viral sore throats improve within a week. See a doctor if it lasts longer or is very severe.
