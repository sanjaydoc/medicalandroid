Tinnitus (Ringing in the Ears)

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Tinnitus is hearing a sound such as ringing, buzzing or humming when there is no external source. It is very common and often harmless, though it can be distressing.

Causes & risk factors

  • Age-related or noise-induced hearing loss
  • Exposure to loud noise or music
  • Ear wax blockage or ear infection
  • Certain medicines
  • High blood pressure or stress
  • Meniere’s disease or other inner-ear problems

Common symptoms

  • Ringing, buzzing or hissing in the ears
  • Sound in one or both ears
  • Muffled hearing
  • Difficulty concentrating or sleeping
  • Dizziness in some cases

Tests & diagnosis

  • Otoscopy (ear examination)
  • Hearing test (audiometry)
  • Blood pressure check
  • Blood tests for anaemia or thyroid
  • MRI in selected cases

Treatment & management

  • Treating any underlying cause such as wax or infection
  • Sound therapy or background noise to mask the ringing
  • Protecting ears from loud noise
  • Managing stress and improving sleep
  • Hearing aids if there is hearing loss
  • Counselling or cognitive therapy for distressing tinnitus

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Tinnitus in only one ear
  • Ringing with sudden hearing loss or dizziness
  • Pulsing tinnitus in time with your heartbeat
  • Tinnitus that seriously affects sleep or mood

Frequently asked questions

Will tinnitus go away?

Many people find it settles or becomes less noticeable over time, especially if a treatable cause is found. Some manage it long term with sound therapy.

Can loud music cause tinnitus?

Yes. Loud music and noise can damage the inner ear and trigger tinnitus. Protecting your ears helps prevent it.
