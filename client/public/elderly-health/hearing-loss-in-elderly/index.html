Hearing loss in the elderly

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Age-related hearing loss is very common and develops gradually, so it is often missed. Treating it with hearing aids improves not just hearing, but also mood, social life and even memory.

What to know

  • Signs: asking people to repeat, high TV volume, struggling in noise
  • Rule out ear wax (a simple, common, reversible cause)
  • A hearing test guides whether hearing aids will help
  • Modern hearing aids are effective and discreet
  • Treating hearing loss helps mood, social life and thinking

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Sudden hearing loss in one or both ears — see a doctor urgently (may be treatable)
  • Hearing loss with ear pain, discharge or dizziness
  • One-sided hearing loss or ringing (needs assessment)
Sudden hearing loss is urgent. Gradual age-related hearing loss should be tested and aided; sudden hearing loss can sometimes be reversed if treated quickly, so seek urgent care. MedDroid gives general information, not a diagnosis.

Frequently asked questions

Is hearing loss a normal part of ageing?

Gradual hearing loss is common with age, but it should still be checked and treated — hearing aids improve quality of life, mood and even thinking.

Can ear wax cause hearing loss?

Yes — wax build-up is a common, easily reversible cause. It is worth ruling out before assuming permanent hearing loss.

When is hearing loss an emergency?

Sudden hearing loss (over hours to days), especially in one ear, needs urgent care — early treatment can sometimes restore it.
