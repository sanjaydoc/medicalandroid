Osteoporosis & bone health

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Bones weaken with age, and osteoporosis makes fractures more likely — often from minor falls. Calcium, vitamin D, exercise and, when needed, medicine keep bones stronger and prevent fractures.

How to protect bones

  • Enough calcium (diet first) and vitamin D
  • Weight-bearing and strength exercise
  • Stop smoking and limit alcohol
  • A bone-density (DEXA) scan to assess risk
  • Bone-strengthening medicines (like bisphosphonates) when advised

When to see a doctor urgently

  • A fracture from a minor fall (suggests osteoporosis — get assessed)
  • Loss of height or a stooped back (possible spine fractures)
  • Sudden severe back pain in an older person
Prevent the first fracture. Osteoporosis is silent until a fracture, so assessing risk and protecting bones early matters. Preventing falls protects fragile bones too. MedDroid gives general information, not a diagnosis.

Frequently asked questions

How can I strengthen bones in old age?

Get enough calcium and vitamin D, do weight-bearing and strength exercise, avoid smoking and excess alcohol, and take bone-strengthening medicine if your doctor advises.

How do I know if I have osteoporosis?

It is usually silent until a fracture. A bone-density (DEXA) scan assesses your risk — recommended for older adults and those with risk factors.

Do I need calcium and vitamin D supplements?

If your diet and sun exposure are not enough — common in older adults — supplements help, especially alongside osteoporosis treatment. Your doctor advises.
