How to stop severe bleeding

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Heavy bleeding can be life-threatening within minutes. The key is firm, direct, continuous pressure on the wound while emergency help is on the way.

Step-by-step

  • 1. Call emergency services for severe bleeding.
  • 2. Apply firm, direct pressure on the wound with a clean cloth or dressing.
  • 3. Keep pressing — do not lift to check; add more dressings on top if it soaks through.
  • 4. If possible, raise the injured part above the level of the heart.
  • 5. Lay the person down and keep them warm (helps prevent shock).
  • 6. For a limb wound that will not stop and is life-threatening, a tourniquet above the wound is a last resort.

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Signs of shock: pale, cold, clammy skin, fast breathing, confusion — call emergency services
  • Do not remove an embedded object — press around it instead
  • Keep pressure on continuously; note the time if a tourniquet is used
Direct pressure is the priority. Firm continuous pressure stops most bleeding. Only use a tourniquet for catastrophic limb bleeding you cannot control. This is not a substitute for emergency care. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop heavy bleeding?

Apply firm, direct, continuous pressure with a clean cloth, add more on top if it soaks through (don’t remove the first), raise the part, and call emergency services.

Should I remove an object stuck in a wound?

No — leave it in place and press firmly around it, as removing it can cause worse bleeding.

When is a tourniquet used?

Only as a last resort for life-threatening limb bleeding that direct pressure cannot control. Apply above the wound, note the time, and get emergency help.
