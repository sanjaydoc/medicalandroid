Stroke first aid: act FAST

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

A stroke is a “brain attack” — blood flow to part of the brain is cut off. Fast treatment can save brain tissue and prevent lasting disability, so recognising it and calling for help immediately is vital.

The FAST test

  • F — Face: ask them to smile. Does one side droop?
  • A — Arms: can they raise both arms? Does one drift down?
  • S — Speech: is it slurred or strange? Can they repeat a simple sentence?
  • T — Time: if you see any of these signs, call emergency services immediately.
  • Note the time symptoms started (it guides treatment).
  • Keep them comfortable; do not give food, drink or medicine.

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Any FAST sign is an emergency — call immediately, even if it seems to improve
  • Other signs: sudden numbness/weakness on one side, sudden confusion, vision loss, severe headache, loss of balance
  • Do not give aspirin (unlike a heart attack — a stroke can be a bleed)
  • Do not let them “sleep it off”
Time lost is brain lost. Clot-busting and other treatments work only within a few hours, so never wait. Do not give aspirin (a stroke may be a bleed). This is not a substitute for emergency care. Call your local emergency number.

Frequently asked questions

What is the FAST test for stroke?

Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services. Any one of these signs means act immediately.

Should I give aspirin for a stroke?

No — unlike a heart attack, a stroke can be caused by bleeding, and aspirin could make it worse. Just call emergency services.

What if the symptoms go away?

Still call for help — it may have been a “mini-stroke” (TIA), which is a serious warning of a possible bigger stroke and needs urgent assessment.
