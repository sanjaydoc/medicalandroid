Medicine for diabetes

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Diabetes medicines are prescribed by a doctor and chosen to fit your sugar levels, weight, kidneys and heart. Metformin is usually first, with other tablets or insulin added as needed — alongside diet and activity.

What to know

  • Metformin is usually the first-line tablet
  • Other tablet classes are added to reach target sugar (HbA1c)
  • Some newer medicines also protect the heart and kidneys
  • Insulin is used in type 1, and in type 2 when needed

Main tablet classes (doctor decides)

  • Metformin (first-line)
  • Sulfonylureas: glimepiride, gliclazide
  • DPP-4 inhibitors: sitagliptin, teneligliptin, linagliptin
  • SGLT2 inhibitors: dapagliflozin, empagliflozin
  • Others: pioglitazone, voglibose

Injectable

  • Insulin (various types)
  • GLP-1 medicines: semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Very high sugar with vomiting or drowsiness (emergency)
  • Low-sugar episodes (shakiness, sweating, confusion) — adjust with your doctor
  • Do not stop diabetes medicine on your own

Misusing antibiotics drives resistance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first medicine for type 2 diabetes?

Metformin is usually first-line, alongside diet and activity. Other tablets or insulin are added to reach and maintain your target sugar (HbA1c).

Which diabetes medicine is best?

It depends on your sugar levels, weight, kidney and heart health. Some newer medicines (SGLT2, GLP-1) also protect the heart and kidneys. Your doctor tailors this.

Can I stop diabetes medicine if my sugar is normal?

Not on your own — normal sugar usually means the medicine is working. Type 2 can sometimes improve with major weight loss, but change medicines only with your doctor.
