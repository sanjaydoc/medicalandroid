Dexamethasone: uses, dose & side effects

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Dexamethasone is a strong, long-acting steroid used for serious inflammation, allergic reactions, some cancers, brain swelling and certain infections. It is potent, so it is dosed carefully.

What it is used for

  • Severe inflammation and allergic reactions
  • Brain swelling and some cancers
  • Certain severe infections (as add-on)
  • Nausea from chemotherapy

Common brand names

  • India: Decdan, Dexona, Wymesone
  • Worldwide: Decadron, Ozurdex (eye)

Common side effects

  • Raised blood sugar
  • Mood and sleep changes
  • Increased appetite, fluid retention

Before taking it — precautions

  • Do not stop abruptly after longer use
  • Monitor blood sugar, especially in diabetes
  • Watch for infections

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Signs of infection (fever, feeling very unwell)
  • Severe mood disturbance
  • Very high blood sugar
Doses are a general reference, not a prescription. Doses shown are typical adult/paediatric references and vary with age, weight, kidney/liver function and the exact product. Paediatric doses are usually weight-based and must be set by a doctor. MedDroid cannot prescribe — always confirm dexamethasone and your dose with a doctor or pharmacist and the leaflet inside the pack.

Frequently asked questions

Is dexamethasone stronger than prednisolone?

Yes — it is more potent and longer-acting, so much smaller doses are used. Your doctor chooses based on the condition.

Is dexamethasone misused for weight/skin?

Yes — unsupervised use for weight gain or fairness is dangerous and can cause serious harm. Only use it as prescribed.
