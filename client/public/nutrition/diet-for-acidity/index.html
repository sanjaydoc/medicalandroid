Diet for acidity & reflux

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Acidity and reflux are often driven by food choices and habits. Identifying your triggers and adjusting how and when you eat can reduce symptoms — often more than medicine alone.

Avoid / helps

  • Common triggers: spicy, fried and fatty foods, citrus, tomato, coffee, chocolate, mint
  • Avoid large meals; eat smaller, more frequent ones
  • Do not lie down for 2–3 hours after eating
  • Helps: bananas, oats, non-citrus fruit, cold milk/curd for some
  • Lose excess weight, and avoid smoking and alcohol

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Difficulty swallowing, weight loss, black stools or vomiting blood — see a doctor (alarm symptoms)
  • Chest pain (rule out the heart)
  • Frequent acidity needing daily medicine — get assessed
Habits matter as much as food. When and how you eat (portion size, late meals, lying down) affects reflux as much as what you eat. Alarm symptoms need a doctor. MedDroid gives general information, not a personalised plan.

Frequently asked questions

What foods cause acidity?

Spicy, fried and fatty foods, citrus, tomato, coffee, chocolate, mint and large or late meals commonly trigger acidity and reflux.

What foods reduce acidity?

Bananas, oats, non-citrus fruit, vegetables and, for some people, cold milk or curd. Smaller, earlier meals help too.

When should acidity be checked?

See a doctor for difficulty swallowing, weight loss, black stools, vomiting blood, or acidity needing daily medicine — these need assessment.
