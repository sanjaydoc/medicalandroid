Dizziness: causes, warning signs & what to do

"Dizzy" can mean light-headedness or a spinning sensation (vertigo). Most causes are benign — inner ear, dehydration, blood pressure — but some need urgent care.

Common causes of dizziness

  • Inner-ear problems (BPPV, labyrinthitis) — spinning vertigo
  • Low blood pressure on standing / dehydration
  • Low blood sugar
  • Anaemia
  • Anxiety or hyperventilation
  • Medication side effects

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Dizziness with slurred speech, face droop, weakness or double vision — call your local emergency number now (stroke)
  • Dizziness with chest pain, fainting or a very fast/slow pulse
  • Sudden severe headache with the dizziness
  • A recent head injury

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between dizziness and vertigo?

Dizziness is a general light-headed feeling; vertigo is a false sense of spinning, usually from the inner ear. The cause and treatment differ.

Why do I feel dizzy when I stand up?

A brief drop in blood pressure on standing (postural hypotension) — often from dehydration or medicines. Rising slowly and drinking fluids helps; persistent cases need review.
