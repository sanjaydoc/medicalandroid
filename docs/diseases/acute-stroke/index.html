Acute Stroke (Brain Attack)

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 25 September 2026

Blood flow to part of the brain is suddenly blocked or a vessel bursts, so brain cells begin to die. It is an emergency — fast treatment can limit damage.

Causes & risk factors

  • A clot blocking a brain artery (ischemic stroke)
  • Bleeding in the brain (hemorrhagic stroke)
  • High blood pressure
  • Atrial fibrillation
  • Diabetes, smoking and high cholesterol

Common symptoms

  • Face drooping on one side
  • Arm or leg weakness or numbness
  • Slurred or confused speech
  • Sudden vision loss
  • Sudden dizziness or loss of balance
  • Sudden severe headache

Tests & diagnosis

  • Urgent CT scan of the brain
  • MRI
  • ECG
  • Blood sugar
  • Clotting tests

Treatment & management

  • Call your local emergency number immediately — time is brain
  • Clot-busting drugs for suitable clot strokes given early
  • Clot removal (thrombectomy) in selected cases
  • Controlling bleeding and blood pressure for hemorrhagic stroke
  • Rehabilitation and prevention of future strokes

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Call your local emergency number now if you notice Face drooping, Arm weakness or Speech difficulty (act FAST)
  • Note the time symptoms started — it guides treatment
  • Do not wait to see if symptoms pass, even a brief episode needs urgent care

Frequently asked questions

What does FAST mean?

Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services — a quick way to spot a stroke.

Why does speed matter so much?

Brain cells die quickly without blood flow, so earlier treatment means less lasting damage.
