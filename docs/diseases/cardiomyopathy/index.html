Cardiomyopathy (Heart Muscle Disease)

A disease of the heart muscle that makes it enlarged, thickened or stiff, so the heart pumps less effectively. Some forms run in families.

Causes & risk factors

  • Genetic / inherited conditions
  • Long-standing high blood pressure
  • Previous heart attack
  • Excess alcohol
  • Viral infections of the heart
  • Sometimes no clear cause

Common symptoms

  • Breathlessness
  • Fatigue
  • Leg and ankle swelling
  • Palpitations
  • Fainting
  • Chest discomfort

Tests & diagnosis

  • Echocardiogram
  • ECG
  • Cardiac MRI
  • Blood tests
  • Genetic testing (selected cases)

Treatment & management

  • Medicines to ease the heart workload and control rhythm
  • Treating the cause where possible (BP, alcohol, infection)
  • Implanted devices such as defibrillators in high-risk patients
  • Limiting strenuous activity if advised
  • Heart transplant in advanced cases

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Call emergency services for fainting, severe breathlessness or chest pain
  • See a cardiologist if you have a family history and any heart symptoms
  • Get checked for unexplained breathlessness or swelling

Frequently asked questions

Is cardiomyopathy inherited?

Some types are; close relatives may be advised to get screened.

Can I exercise with cardiomyopathy?

Light activity is often fine, but competitive or intense exercise may need to be limited — always follow your cardiologist advice.
