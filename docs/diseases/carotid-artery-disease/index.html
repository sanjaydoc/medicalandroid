Carotid Artery Disease

Narrowing of the carotid arteries in the neck that supply the brain, from plaque build-up. It is an important, treatable cause of stroke.

Causes & risk factors

  • Atherosclerosis
  • High blood pressure and cholesterol
  • Smoking
  • Diabetes
  • Ageing

Common symptoms

  • Often no symptoms until a warning stroke
  • Sudden weakness or numbness on one side
  • Slurred speech
  • Sudden vision loss in one eye
  • Dizziness

Tests & diagnosis

  • Carotid Doppler ultrasound
  • CT or MR angiography
  • Lipid profile
  • Blood sugar

Treatment & management

  • Stopping smoking and controlling BP, cholesterol and diabetes
  • Blood thinners / antiplatelet medicines
  • Cholesterol-lowering medicines (statins)
  • Surgery to clear the artery (carotid endarterectomy) for severe narrowing
  • Carotid stenting in selected patients

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Call emergency services immediately for face droop, arm weakness or speech problems (stroke signs)
  • See a doctor if a neck bruit (sound) is found
  • Seek urgent care for brief episodes of weakness or vision loss (mini-stroke)

Frequently asked questions

What is a mini-stroke?

A transient ischemic attack (TIA) — brief stroke-like symptoms that recover but warn of a high stroke risk and need urgent assessment.

Does narrowing always need surgery?

No — mild narrowing is managed with medicines and lifestyle; surgery is for severe, especially symptomatic, narrowing.
