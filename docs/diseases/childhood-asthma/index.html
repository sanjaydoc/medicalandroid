Childhood Asthma

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 25 September 2026

Asthma makes the airways narrow and inflamed, causing wheeze, cough and breathlessness. With the right inhalers and a plan, most children lead full, active lives.

Causes & risk factors

  • A tendency to allergy (often runs in families)
  • Triggers: colds, dust, pollen, pets, smoke and pollution
  • Exercise or cold air
  • Air pollution — a big factor in many Indian cities

Common symptoms

  • Wheezing
  • Cough, often worse at night
  • Breathlessness
  • Chest tightness
  • Symptoms worse with colds, exercise or triggers

Tests & diagnosis

  • Clinical assessment and history
  • Peak flow / spirometry (older children)
  • CBC (allergy markers)
  • Chest X-ray (in selected cases)

Treatment & management

  • Reliever inhaler for symptoms, usually with a spacer
  • Preventer inhaler daily if symptoms are frequent
  • A written asthma action plan for home and school
  • Identify and reduce triggers (smoke, dust, pollution)
  • Regular review to keep control and check inhaler technique

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Severe breathlessness, unable to speak in full sentences, or blue lips — seek urgent care
  • Reliever inhaler not helping or needed very often — urgent
  • Fast breathing or ribs pulling in — urgent
  • Frequent night-time or activity-limiting symptoms — review with the doctor

Frequently asked questions

Will my child grow out of asthma?

Some children have fewer symptoms as they grow, especially milder cases, while others continue into adulthood. Good control keeps them active and protects their airways in the meantime.

Are inhalers safe for children?

Yes. Inhalers deliver small doses directly to the lungs and are the safest, most effective treatment. Using a spacer improves delivery. Your doctor will show the correct technique.
