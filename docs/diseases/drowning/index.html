Drowning

Breathing difficulty from being submerged in water, which can rapidly deprive the body of oxygen. Fast rescue and resuscitation are life-saving.

Causes & risk factors

  • Swimming without supervision
  • Weak or non-swimmers in deep water
  • Alcohol near water
  • Seizures or fainting in water
  • Floods and open water bodies

Common symptoms

  • Coughing and breathing difficulty
  • Choking or gasping
  • Unconsciousness
  • No breathing (severe)
  • Chest discomfort

Tests & diagnosis

  • Oxygen level (pulse oximetry)
  • Chest X-ray
  • Electrolytes

Treatment & management

  • Call your local emergency number immediately
  • Get the person out of the water safely without endangering yourself
  • Start CPR if they are not breathing normally
  • Give oxygen and hospital monitoring, as lung problems can appear later
  • Keep the person warm

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Call your local emergency number now for any near-drowning, even if the person seems to recover
  • Start CPR immediately if breathing has stopped
  • Always go to hospital, as breathing problems can develop hours later

Frequently asked questions

Why go to hospital if the person seems fine?

Water in the lungs can cause breathing problems hours later, so monitoring is important after any drowning incident.

Should I dive in to rescue someone?

Only if it is safe — reach or throw something to them first, so you do not become a second victim.
