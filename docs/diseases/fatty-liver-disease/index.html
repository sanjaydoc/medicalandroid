Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD)

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is fat build-up in the liver not caused by alcohol. It is now the commonest liver condition and is linked to weight and diabetes.

Causes & risk factors

  • Being overweight, especially around the tummy
  • Type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance
  • High triglycerides and cholesterol
  • A diet high in sugar and refined carbohydrates
  • Metabolic syndrome

Common symptoms

  • Often none in the early stages
  • Tiredness
  • A dull ache in the upper right tummy
  • Discomfort or fullness
  • Swelling in advanced disease

Tests & diagnosis

  • LFT
  • Liver ultrasound
  • FibroScan (to measure scarring)
  • Blood sugar and HbA1c
  • Lipid profile

Treatment & management

  • Gradual weight loss — losing 7-10% of body weight can reverse early disease
  • A balanced diet low in sugar and refined carbs
  • Regular exercise
  • Tightly control diabetes, cholesterol and blood pressure
  • Avoid alcohol and unnecessary medicines that stress the liver

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Yellow eyes or skin
  • Swelling of the tummy or legs
  • Easy bruising, bleeding or confusion
  • Abnormal liver blood tests on a health check

Frequently asked questions

Can fatty liver turn into cirrhosis?

In some people the liver becomes inflamed (NASH) and gradually scars, which can progress to cirrhosis. Early lifestyle change greatly reduces this risk.

Is there a medicine for fatty liver?

The mainstay is weight loss and controlling diabetes and cholesterol. Some medicines are being studied, but lifestyle change remains the most effective treatment.
