Heart Valve Replacement Surgery

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 22 September 2026

Surgery to replace a badly damaged heart valve with a mechanical or tissue valve when repair is not possible. It restores normal blood flow through the heart.

Causes & risk factors

  • Severe valve narrowing (stenosis)
  • Severe valve leakage (regurgitation)
  • Rheumatic valve damage
  • Infected valve (endocarditis)
  • Congenital valve defects

Common symptoms

  • Breathlessness
  • Fatigue
  • Chest discomfort
  • Leg swelling
  • Fainting

Tests & diagnosis

  • Echocardiogram
  • ECG
  • Coronary angiography
  • Pre-operative blood tests
  • PT/INR (for mechanical valves)

Treatment & management

  • Mechanical valve (durable, needs lifelong blood thinners)
  • Tissue valve (fewer blood thinners, may wear out sooner)
  • Minimally invasive or catheter-based replacement (such as TAVI) in selected patients
  • Cardiac rehabilitation after surgery
  • Regular follow-up and, for mechanical valves, INR monitoring

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Call emergency services for chest pain, fever or sudden breathlessness after surgery
  • See your doctor for unusual bleeding if on blood thinners
  • Report fever or new murmurs promptly (possible valve infection)

Frequently asked questions

Mechanical or tissue valve — which is better?

Mechanical valves last longer but need lifelong blood thinners; tissue valves need fewer but may wear out. The choice depends on age and lifestyle.

Do I need antibiotics before dental work?

Many valve patients do, to prevent valve infection — check with your cardiologist.
