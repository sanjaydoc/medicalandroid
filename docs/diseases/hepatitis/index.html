Viral Hepatitis

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Hepatitis means inflammation of the liver. Viral hepatitis (types A, B, C, D and E) is the commonest cause. Some types are short-lived; others can become long-term.

Causes & risk factors

  • Hepatitis A and E — from contaminated food or water
  • Hepatitis B and C — through blood, unsafe injections or from mother to baby
  • Sharing needles or unsterile procedures
  • Unprotected sex (hepatitis B)
  • Alcohol and some medicines can also inflame the liver

Common symptoms

  • Yellowing of the eyes and skin (jaundice)
  • Tiredness
  • Nausea and loss of appetite
  • Discomfort in the upper right tummy
  • Dark urine and pale stools
  • Mild fever

Tests & diagnosis

  • LFT
  • Hepatitis B (HBsAg)
  • Hepatitis A, C and E antibody tests
  • Bilirubin
  • Liver ultrasound

Treatment & management

  • Hepatitis A and E usually settle with rest, fluids and good nutrition
  • Hepatitis C is now curable with modern antiviral tablets
  • Hepatitis B may need long-term antiviral medicine
  • Avoid alcohol and unnecessary medicines that stress the liver
  • Vaccination prevents hepatitis A and B

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Yellow eyes or skin with dark urine
  • Persistent vomiting or being unable to keep fluids down
  • Confusion, drowsiness or easy bruising
  • Severe tummy pain or swelling

Frequently asked questions

Is hepatitis contagious?

Some types are. Hepatitis A and E spread through contaminated food or water; B and C spread through blood and body fluids. Vaccines and hygiene reduce the risk.

Can hepatitis be prevented?

Yes — vaccines protect against A and B, and safe food, clean water, sterile needles and safe blood transfusions prevent the others.
