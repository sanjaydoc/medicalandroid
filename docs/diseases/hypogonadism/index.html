Hypogonadism

Male hypogonadism is when the testes do not produce enough testosterone or sperm. It can be present from birth or develop later, causing low hormone effects and fertility problems.

Causes & risk factors

  • Testicular problems (injury, infection, undescended testes)
  • Pituitary or hypothalamus problems affecting hormone signals
  • Genetic conditions (such as Klinefelter syndrome)
  • Obesity, diabetes and chronic illness
  • Certain medicines and cancer treatments

Common symptoms

  • Low sex drive
  • Erection difficulties
  • Tiredness
  • Reduced muscle mass and body hair
  • Infertility
  • Delayed puberty in adolescents

Tests & diagnosis

  • Testosterone
  • LH and FSH
  • Prolactin
  • Semen analysis
  • Genetic tests in selected cases
  • MRI (pituitary) if indicated

Treatment & management

  • Testosterone replacement when fertility is not the goal
  • Hormone treatments to stimulate sperm production if fertility is wanted
  • Treating the underlying cause (weight, pituitary problem)
  • Monitoring during treatment
  • Support for bone health and mood

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Low libido, tiredness and reduced body hair together
  • Delayed puberty in a teenager
  • Infertility with low testosterone
  • Symptoms with a known pituitary or testicular condition

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between hypogonadism and low testosterone?

Hypogonadism is the underlying condition where the testes underfunction; low testosterone is one of its main results. Tests of LH and FSH help find where the problem lies.

Can hypogonadism be treated if I want children?

Yes, but plain testosterone lowers sperm production, so fertility-preserving hormone treatments are used instead when a man wants to conceive.
