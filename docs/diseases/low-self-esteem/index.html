Low Self-Esteem

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Low self-esteem means having a poor opinion of yourself, which can affect confidence, relationships and mood. It can be improved with support and by changing unhelpful thinking patterns.

Causes & risk factors

  • Critical or difficult experiences, especially in childhood
  • Bullying or ongoing criticism
  • Stressful life events or failures
  • Depression or anxiety
  • Comparing yourself with others, including on social media

Common symptoms

  • Being very self-critical
  • Difficulty accepting compliments
  • Avoiding challenges or new things
  • Feeling not good enough
  • Low mood and anxiety
  • Difficulty setting boundaries

Tests & diagnosis

  • Usually none — this is about support
  • Assessment if depression or anxiety is present

Treatment & management

  • Talking therapy such as cognitive behavioural therapy
  • Challenging unhelpful, self-critical thoughts
  • Setting small, achievable goals to build confidence
  • Practising self-compassion and self-care
  • Reducing unhelpful comparisons and social media

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Thoughts of harming yourself — get help immediately: contact a crisis line or call your local emergency number now
  • Low self-esteem linked to persistent low mood or anxiety
  • Self-esteem issues affecting work, study or relationships
  • Feeling stuck despite trying to improve things yourself

Frequently asked questions

Can low self-esteem be improved?

Yes. Self-esteem can grow with practice and support. Therapy, especially CBT, helps you notice and change self-critical thinking, build confidence through small steps, and treat yourself more kindly.

Is low self-esteem the same as depression?

They are linked but different. Low self-esteem is a negative view of yourself; depression is a persistent low mood affecting many areas of life. Low self-esteem can contribute to depression, and both can be helped.
