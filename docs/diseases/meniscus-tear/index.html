Meniscus Tear

The meniscus is a C-shaped cartilage cushion in the knee. It can tear during twisting movements in younger people, or gradually wear and tear with age.

Causes & risk factors

  • Twisting the knee while the foot is planted
  • Deep squatting or kneeling
  • Sports and sudden pivots
  • Age-related cartilage weakening
  • Heavy lifting with a bent knee

Common symptoms

  • Knee pain, often along the joint line
  • Swelling that develops over a day or two
  • Clicking, catching or locking of the knee
  • Difficulty fully straightening the knee
  • Knee feeling unstable

Tests & diagnosis

  • MRI
  • X-ray
  • Clinical examination (McMurray test)
  • Knee arthroscopy

Treatment & management

  • Rest, ice and activity modification
  • Physiotherapy to strengthen the knee
  • Anti-inflammatory pain relief as advised
  • Arthroscopic repair or trimming for persistent locking
  • Gradual return to activity

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Knee locked in one position and unable to straighten
  • Severe swelling or inability to bear weight
  • Repeated giving way of the knee
  • Pain that does not settle with rest

Frequently asked questions

Can a meniscus tear heal without surgery?

Small tears in the outer, well-supplied part of the meniscus can heal with rest and physiotherapy. Tears in the inner part have poor blood supply and may need surgery.

Is it safe to keep walking with a meniscus tear?

Gentle walking is usually fine, but avoid deep squats, twisting and heavy loading until you have been assessed, as these can worsen the tear.
