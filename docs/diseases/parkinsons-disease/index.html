Parkinson's Disease

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 25 September 2026

Parkinson's disease is a progressive brain condition where cells that make dopamine are lost, leading to tremor, stiffness and slow movement. Symptoms develop gradually over years.

Causes & risk factors

  • Loss of dopamine-producing brain cells (cause often unknown)
  • Older age
  • Genetic factors in some families
  • Possible environmental exposures
  • More common in men

Common symptoms

  • Tremor, often in a hand at rest
  • Slowness of movement
  • Muscle stiffness
  • Balance problems and shuffling walk
  • Small, cramped handwriting
  • Soft or slurred speech
  • Constipation

Tests & diagnosis

  • Clinical diagnosis (history and exam)
  • MRI brain (to exclude other causes)
  • DaTscan (dopamine imaging)
  • Response to Parkinson medicine

Treatment & management

  • Medicines that boost or mimic dopamine to ease symptoms
  • Physiotherapy for movement, balance and posture
  • Speech and occupational therapy
  • Regular exercise to maintain mobility
  • Advanced options such as deep brain stimulation in selected cases

When to see a doctor urgently

  • A new tremor, stiffness or slowness of movement
  • Falls, freezing when walking or worsening balance
  • Difficulty swallowing or choking
  • Sudden confusion, hallucinations or a rapid change in symptoms

Frequently asked questions

Is Parkinson's disease fatal?

Parkinson's itself is not usually a direct cause of death, and treatment can control symptoms for many years. It is a long-term condition that needs ongoing care as it progresses.

Does everyone with a tremor have Parkinson’s?

No. Many tremors are due to other causes, such as essential tremor or anxiety. A Parkinson tremor is typically most noticeable at rest. A doctor can tell them apart.
