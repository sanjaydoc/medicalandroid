PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)

PCOS is a common hormone condition in women of reproductive age causing irregular periods, excess male-type hormones and, often, difficulty with ovulation. It is closely linked to insulin resistance.

Causes & risk factors

  • Insulin resistance
  • Higher levels of male-type hormones (androgens)
  • Genetic and family tendency
  • Excess weight, which worsens the hormone imbalance

Common symptoms

  • Irregular or missed periods
  • Acne
  • Excess facial or body hair
  • Weight gain
  • Difficulty getting pregnant
  • Hair thinning on the scalp
  • Tiredness

Tests & diagnosis

  • TSH
  • Testosterone
  • Fasting sugar
  • HbA1c
  • Pelvic ultrasound
  • LH, FSH and prolactin

Treatment & management

  • Weight loss and exercise, which improve periods and fertility
  • Hormonal treatment to regulate periods
  • Medicines to improve insulin sensitivity where indicated
  • Treatments for acne and excess hair
  • Ovulation support when trying to conceive

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Periods that are absent or very irregular
  • Difficulty conceiving after trying for a year
  • Troublesome acne or excess hair growth
  • Very heavy or prolonged bleeding

Frequently asked questions

Can women with PCOS get pregnant?

Yes. Many conceive naturally or with help such as weight loss and ovulation-inducing treatment. Early advice improves the chances.

Is PCOS linked to diabetes?

Yes. Insulin resistance is central to PCOS, raising the risk of prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, so blood sugar should be checked periodically.
