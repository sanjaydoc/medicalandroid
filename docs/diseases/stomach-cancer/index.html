Stomach (Gastric) Cancer

Stomach cancer often causes vague symptoms early on, such as indigestion, so it can be mistaken for something minor. Persistent tummy symptoms in older adults deserve a check.

Causes & risk factors

  • Long-term Helicobacter pylori infection
  • A diet high in salted, smoked or pickled foods
  • Smoking
  • Family history of stomach cancer
  • Long-standing stomach inflammation (chronic gastritis)
  • Being older and male

Common symptoms

  • Persistent indigestion or heartburn
  • Upper tummy pain or discomfort
  • Feeling full quickly when eating
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Black stools or blood in vomit

Tests & diagnosis

  • Upper endoscopy (gastroscopy) with biopsy
  • Full blood count (checks for anaemia)
  • CT scan for staging
  • Test for Helicobacter pylori

Treatment & management

  • Surgery to remove part or all of the stomach
  • Chemotherapy before or after surgery
  • Radiotherapy in some cases
  • Targeted therapy for certain cancers
  • Treating H. pylori lowers future risk

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Indigestion that persists or gets worse, especially over 50
  • Difficulty swallowing, or feeling full very quickly
  • Black stools, vomiting blood or unexplained weight loss

Frequently asked questions

Is indigestion a sign of stomach cancer?

Usually not — indigestion is very common and mostly harmless. But if it is persistent, new in an older adult, or comes with weight loss or swallowing trouble, it should be assessed.

Can an ulcer bug cause cancer?

Long-term infection with Helicobacter pylori raises the risk of stomach cancer. It can be tested for and treated, which reduces that risk.
