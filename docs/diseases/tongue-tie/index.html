Tongue Tie

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 25 September 2026

Tongue tie is when the strip of tissue under the tongue is short or tight, limiting movement. Many babies feed fine, but some need a quick, simple procedure.

Causes & risk factors

  • A short or tight band of tissue under the tongue (present at birth)
  • More common in boys
  • Can run in families
  • Usually no identifiable cause

Common symptoms

  • Difficulty latching or staying latched at the breast
  • Feeding for a long time or seeming unsatisfied
  • Poor weight gain
  • The tongue cannot poke out past the lips or lift well
  • A heart-shaped tongue tip when crying
  • Sore nipples for the breastfeeding mother

Tests & diagnosis

  • Examination of the tongue and feeding assessment

Treatment & management

  • Many babies need no treatment and feed well
  • Feeding support and positioning advice first
  • A quick, simple division of the tie (frenotomy) if feeding is affected
  • The baby can usually feed straight afterwards
  • Speech review later only if problems arise as they grow

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Ongoing feeding difficulty or poor weight gain — get a feeding assessment
  • Persistent sore nipples affecting breastfeeding
  • A baby not settling and always hungry after feeds
  • Later speech concerns linked to tongue movement

Frequently asked questions

Does tongue tie always need treatment?

No. Many babies with tongue tie feed and develop normally and need nothing done. Treatment is considered mainly when it clearly interferes with feeding.

Is the procedure painful?

A frenotomy is quick and simple, often with little or no bleeding, and babies can usually feed straight away, which soothes them. It is generally very well tolerated.
