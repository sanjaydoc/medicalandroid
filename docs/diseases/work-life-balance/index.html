Work-Life Balance

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

A poor balance between work and personal life can leave you exhausted, stressed and disconnected from what matters. Small changes and support can help you find a healthier rhythm.

Causes & risk factors

  • Long working hours or an always-on culture
  • Difficulty saying no or setting boundaries
  • Financial pressure
  • Caring responsibilities
  • Perfectionism or high expectations
  • Poor planning or time management

Common symptoms

  • Constant tiredness
  • Feeling overwhelmed
  • Little time for rest or relationships
  • Irritability or low mood
  • Poor sleep
  • Neglecting your own health

Tests & diagnosis

  • Usually none — this is about support
  • Assessment if stress, burnout or low mood develops

Treatment & management

  • Setting clear boundaries between work and personal time
  • Prioritising rest, relationships and activities you enjoy
  • Improving planning and delegating where possible
  • Talking to your employer about workload if needed
  • Counselling or coaching for ongoing difficulties

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Thoughts of harming yourself — get help immediately: contact a crisis line or call your local emergency number now
  • Signs of burnout or persistent exhaustion
  • Stress affecting your health, sleep or relationships
  • Feeling unable to switch off or cope
Not a diagnosis. MedDroid gives general educational information about work-life balance. It cannot examine you, diagnose or prescribe — always confirm with a qualified doctor.

Frequently asked questions

How do I set boundaries with work?

Start by defining clear finish times, turning off work notifications outside hours, and protecting time for rest and relationships. Small, consistent changes add up, and talking to your manager can help.

When should I get help?

If poor balance is causing burnout, persistent stress, or affecting your health, sleep or relationships, it is worth reaching out to a counsellor or your doctor before it worsens.
