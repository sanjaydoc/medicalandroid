Sleep problems in the elderly

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Sleep naturally becomes lighter and more broken with age. Good sleep habits help most older adults — and sleeping pills are best avoided, as they raise the risk of falls and confusion.

What helps

  • Keep a regular sleep-wake schedule and get daytime light and activity
  • Limit long daytime naps, caffeine and evening fluids
  • A calm, dark, comfortable bedroom
  • Treat causes: pain, frequent urination, anxiety, sleep apnoea
  • Avoid sleeping pills where possible (fall and confusion risk)

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Loud snoring with daytime sleepiness (possible sleep apnoea)
  • Sleep problems with low mood (possible depression)
  • Sleeping pills causing confusion, unsteadiness or falls
  • Sudden change in sleep with confusion (possible illness)
Habits over pills — especially in the elderly. Sleeping pills raise the risk of falls, confusion and dependence in older adults, so sleep-hygiene and treating causes come first. MedDroid gives general information, not a diagnosis.

Frequently asked questions

Why do older people sleep less?

Sleep naturally becomes lighter and more fragmented with age, and pain, urination, medicines and anxiety can add to it. Good habits help most.

Are sleeping pills safe for the elderly?

They carry extra risks in older adults — falls, confusion and dependence — so they are avoided where possible in favour of sleep-hygiene and treating causes.

How can an elderly person sleep better?

Regular schedule, daytime activity and light, limited naps and caffeine, a comfortable dark room, and treating pain or frequent urination.
