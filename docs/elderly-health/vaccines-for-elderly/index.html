Vaccines for the elderly

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Vaccines are not just for children. As immunity weakens with age, certain vaccines protect older adults from serious illnesses like flu, pneumonia and shingles — preventing hospitalisation and worse.

Commonly recommended (ask your doctor)

  • Influenza (flu) — yearly
  • Pneumococcal (pneumonia) vaccine
  • Shingles (herpes zoster) vaccine
  • Tetanus/diphtheria booster
  • COVID-19 vaccination/boosters as advised

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Postpone routine vaccines if seriously unwell (a mild cold is usually fine)
  • A severe reaction to a previous dose — tell your doctor
  • Difficulty breathing or swelling after a vaccine (very rare) — emergency
Vaccines prevent serious illness in older age. Recommended vaccines vary by country and health condition, so confirm with your doctor. They are a safe, effective way to prevent serious infections in older adults. MedDroid gives general information, not a diagnosis.

Frequently asked questions

Which vaccines do elderly people need?

Commonly the yearly flu vaccine, pneumococcal (pneumonia), shingles, a tetanus booster, and COVID-19 as advised. Your doctor tailors this to your health and country.

Is the flu vaccine necessary every year?

Yes — flu strains change and immunity fades, so a yearly flu vaccine is recommended for older adults, who are at higher risk of complications.

What is the shingles vaccine?

It reduces the risk of shingles (a painful reactivation of the chickenpox virus) and its lasting nerve pain, and is recommended for older adults.
