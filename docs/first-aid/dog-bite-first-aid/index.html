Dog bite first aid & rabies prevention

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Any bite or scratch from a dog (or cat, monkey or bat) can transmit rabies, which is almost always fatal once symptoms start — but completely preventable with prompt washing and vaccination.

Step-by-step

  • 1. Wash the wound immediately with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes.
  • 2. Apply an antiseptic (like povidone-iodine or alcohol).
  • 3. Do not close/stitch the wound tightly (unless a doctor decides).
  • 4. Go to a doctor or hospital the same day for anti-rabies vaccination.
  • 5. You may also need rabies immunoglobulin for deep or high-risk bites.
  • 6. Check that your tetanus vaccination is up to date.

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Rabies is preventable but almost always fatal once symptoms appear — never skip vaccination
  • Deep bites, bites to the head/neck/hands, or an unknown/wild animal are higher risk
  • Signs of wound infection: spreading redness, pus, fever
  • Do not rely on traditional remedies instead of vaccination
Wash long, then vaccinate — same day. Thorough washing plus prompt anti-rabies vaccination prevents rabies. Never wait or use only home remedies. This is not a substitute for medical care.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an injection after a dog bite?

Yes — wash the wound thoroughly and get anti-rabies vaccination the same day. Deep or high-risk bites may also need rabies immunoglobulin and a tetanus check.

What if the dog looks healthy or is vaccinated?

Still get medical advice and washing done — a healthy-looking animal can carry rabies, and doctors decide on vaccination based on the risk.

How long do I have to get vaccinated?

As soon as possible — ideally the same day. The vaccine works best when started promptly after exposure.
