Medicine for constipation

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Constipation usually improves with more fluids, fibre and activity. When a laxative is needed, gentler types (fibre and osmotic) are preferred for regular use, with stimulant laxatives kept short-term.

What to know

  • First: more water, fibre, fruit and activity
  • Fibre (bulk) laxatives: gentle, for regular use with water
  • Osmotic laxatives (lactulose, PEG): draw water into the bowel
  • Stimulant laxatives (senna, bisacodyl): short-term only

Common over-the-counter options

  • Ispaghula/psyllium (fibre)
  • Lactulose, polyethylene glycol (PEG) — osmotic
  • Senna, bisacodyl — stimulant (short-term)
  • Glycerine suppositories

Doctor-guided

  • Treatment of an underlying cause
  • Newer prescription options for chronic constipation

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Constipation with severe pain, vomiting and a swollen belly (possible obstruction)
  • Blood in the stool, or unexplained weight loss
  • A sudden change in bowel habit, especially over 50

Frequently asked questions

What is the best laxative for constipation?

Fibre and osmotic laxatives (like ispaghula, lactulose or PEG) are gentle and suit regular use; stimulant laxatives (senna, bisacodyl) work faster but are for short-term use.

Are laxatives safe for daily use?

Gentle fibre and osmotic laxatives can be used longer term under guidance; stimulant laxatives are best short-term. Diet and fluids should come first.

When should constipation be checked?

With severe pain and vomiting, blood in the stool, weight loss, or a new change in bowel habit — especially over 50.
