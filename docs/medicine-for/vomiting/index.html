Medicine for vomiting & nausea

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

For most vomiting, sips of fluid or ORS and rest are enough. Anti-sickness medicines help in some situations, but the cause matters — and persistent vomiting needs a doctor.

What to know

  • Small, frequent sips of fluid or ORS prevent dehydration
  • Anti-sickness medicines can help but need care in children
  • Ginger and rest help mild nausea
  • Treat the cause (infection, motion sickness, pregnancy)

Commonly used (doctor-guided)

  • Ondansetron (strong anti-sickness)
  • Domperidone (nausea with bloating)
  • Doxylamine + B6 for pregnancy nausea
  • Antihistamines for motion sickness (dimenhydrinate, meclizine)

First, always

  • ORS / rehydration
  • Rest and small sips

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Green (bile) or blood-stained vomit
  • Signs of dehydration, or unable to keep any fluids down
  • Vomiting with severe tummy pain, stiff neck, or after a head injury
Not a prescription. This lists medicines commonly used for this condition to help you understand your options — it is NOT a prescription. Do not self-medicate: the right choice and dose depend on the cause, your health and other medicines, and prescription drugs (especially antibiotics) need a doctor. Misusing antibiotics drives resistance.

Frequently asked questions

What medicine stops vomiting?

Ondansetron and domperidone are commonly used anti-sickness medicines, but the cause matters and children need caution. Rehydration with ORS comes first.

What can I take for vomiting in pregnancy?

Doxylamine with vitamin B6 is considered safe for pregnancy nausea; severe vomiting needs a doctor. Ginger and small frequent meals also help.

When is vomiting serious?

Green or bloody vomit, dehydration, inability to keep fluids down, severe pain, stiff neck, or vomiting after a head injury all need urgent care.
