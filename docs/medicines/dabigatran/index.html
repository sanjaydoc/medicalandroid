Dabigatran: uses, side effects & brands

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Dabigatran is a newer fixed-dose blood thinner for atrial fibrillation and clots. It belongs to the Blood thinner (anticoagulant) group. These reduce the blood’s tendency to clot, preventing strokes and dangerous clots.

What it is used for

  • Preventing stroke in atrial fibrillation
  • Treating and preventing deep-vein clots and lung clots
  • After certain heart valve or clot conditions

Common brand names

  • India: Pradaxa, Dabimet
  • Worldwide: Pradaxa

Common side effects

  • Easier bruising or bleeding
  • Nosebleeds or gum bleeding
  • Heavier periods

Before taking it — precautions

  • Tell every doctor/dentist you take a blood thinner
  • Warfarin needs regular blood (INR) tests and diet consistency
  • Avoid unnecessary NSAIDs (bleeding risk)

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Black stools, blood in urine/vomit, or coughing blood
  • A severe headache or a bad fall (internal bleeding)
  • Bleeding that will not stop

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between warfarin and newer blood thinners?

Warfarin needs regular INR blood tests and diet care; newer ones (like apixaban, rivaroxaban) have fixed doses and no routine testing, but are not suitable for every condition.

Can I take painkillers with a blood thinner?

Paracetamol is usually preferred; avoid NSAIDs like ibuprofen unless your doctor agrees, as they add to bleeding risk.
