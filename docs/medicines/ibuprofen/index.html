Ibuprofen (Brufen): uses, dose & side effects

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Ibuprofen is an anti-inflammatory painkiller (NSAID) for pain, swelling and fever. It works well but is harder on the stomach and kidneys than paracetamol, so it is used more carefully.

Typical dose — general reference only, NOT a prescription

MeasureRange
CategoryOver-the-counter (OTC)
Adult dose200–400 mg every 4–6 hours with food; OTC max 1200 mg/day (up to 2400 mg/day under a doctor)
Child (paediatric) dose5–10 mg/kg per dose every 6–8 h (max 40 mg/kg/day); avoid under 3 months

What it is used for

  • Pain with inflammation (sprains, back pain, arthritis)
  • Period pain
  • Headache and dental pain
  • Fever

Common brand names

  • India: Brufen, Ibugesic, Combiflam (with paracetamol)
  • Worldwide: Advil, Motrin, Nurofen

Common side effects

  • Stomach upset, heartburn or indigestion
  • Nausea
  • Raised blood pressure with long-term use

Before taking it — precautions

  • Take with or after food
  • Avoid with ulcers, kidney disease or certain heart conditions
  • Caution in asthma, late pregnancy and the elderly

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Black/tarry stools or vomiting blood (stomach bleeding)
  • Severe stomach pain
  • Swelling, wheezing or rash (allergy)
Doses are a general reference, not a prescription. Doses shown are typical adult/paediatric references and vary with age, weight, kidney/liver function and the exact product. Paediatric doses are usually weight-based and must be set by a doctor. MedDroid cannot prescribe — always confirm ibuprofen and your dose with a doctor or pharmacist and the leaflet inside the pack.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take ibuprofen and paracetamol together?

Yes, they can often be combined or alternated for stronger relief as they work differently. Check with a pharmacist if you have other conditions.

Should ibuprofen be taken with food?

Yes — with or after food to reduce stomach irritation.

Who should avoid ibuprofen?

People with ulcers, kidney disease, some heart problems, or in late pregnancy should avoid it or use it only on medical advice.
