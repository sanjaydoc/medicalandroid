Pregabalin (Lyrica): uses, dose & side effects

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Pregabalin calms overactive nerve signals. It is used for nerve pain, some seizures and anxiety. It can cause dependence, so it is prescribed and stopped carefully.

Typical dose — general reference only, NOT a prescription

MeasureRange
CategoryPrescription
Adult dose75 mg twice daily, up to 300 mg/day (max 600 mg); tapered when stopping
Child (paediatric) doseNot established in children

What it is used for

  • Nerve pain (diabetic neuropathy, sciatica, shingles pain)
  • Some seizure types (add-on)
  • Generalised anxiety (in some countries)

Common brand names

  • India: Pregeb, Pregabid, Nervmax, Maxgalin
  • Worldwide: Lyrica

Common side effects

  • Drowsiness and dizziness
  • Weight gain
  • Blurred vision or swelling

Before taking it — precautions

  • Do not drive until you know how it affects you
  • Do not stop suddenly (taper)
  • Avoid alcohol; caution with other sedatives

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Severe drowsiness or breathing problems (especially with opioids/sedatives)
  • Mood changes or thoughts of self-harm
  • Signs of allergy
Doses are a general reference, not a prescription. Doses shown are typical adult/paediatric references and vary with age, weight, kidney/liver function and the exact product. Paediatric doses are usually weight-based and must be set by a doctor. MedDroid cannot prescribe — always confirm pregabalin and your dose with a doctor or pharmacist and the leaflet inside the pack.

Frequently asked questions

Is pregabalin addictive?

It can cause dependence and is sometimes misused, so it is prescribed carefully, at controlled doses, and tapered rather than stopped suddenly.

Why does pregabalin make me sleepy?

It calms nerve activity, which commonly causes drowsiness and dizziness, especially at first. Avoid driving until you know your response.
