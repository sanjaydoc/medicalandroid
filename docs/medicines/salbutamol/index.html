Salbutamol / Albuterol (Asthalin): uses, dose & side effects

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Salbutamol (called albuterol in the USA) is a reliever inhaler that quickly opens tight airways in asthma. It eases wheeze and breathlessness within minutes but does not treat the underlying inflammation.

Typical dose — general reference only, NOT a prescription

MeasureRange
CategoryPrescription
Adult doseInhaler 100 mcg: 1–2 puffs as needed for symptoms (and before exercise)
Child (paediatric) dose1–2 puffs as needed via inhaler with a spacer; dose set by doctor

What it is used for

  • Quick relief of asthma symptoms (wheeze, breathlessness)
  • Before exercise to prevent symptoms
  • COPD flare-ups

Common brand names

  • India: Asthalin, Levolin (levosalbutamol), Aerocort
  • Worldwide: Ventolin, ProAir, Proventil (albuterol)

Common side effects

  • Shakiness or tremor
  • Fast heartbeat
  • Headache

Before taking it — precautions

  • Use a spacer for better delivery, especially in children
  • Needing it often means asthma is not controlled — see your doctor
  • Carry it at all times if asthmatic

When to see a doctor urgently

  • Reliever not working, or needing it very often — urgent asthma review
  • Severe breathlessness, blue lips, unable to speak — emergency
Doses are a general reference, not a prescription. Doses shown are typical adult/paediatric references and vary with age, weight, kidney/liver function and the exact product. Paediatric doses are usually weight-based and must be set by a doctor. MedDroid cannot prescribe — always confirm salbutamol / albuterol and your dose with a doctor or pharmacist and the leaflet inside the pack.

Frequently asked questions

How often can I use my salbutamol inhaler?

For quick relief as needed, but if you need it more than a couple of times a week your asthma control needs reviewing. Overuse is a warning sign.

Is salbutamol a steroid?

No — it is a fast-acting bronchodilator (reliever). Preventer inhalers, which are often steroids, treat the underlying inflammation.
