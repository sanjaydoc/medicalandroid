Testicular lump: when to worry

Written & medically reviewed by Dr. Sanjay Anbu, MBBS · Last reviewed 26 September 2026

Most testicular lumps are harmless (like cysts), but a painless lump can be the first sign of testicular cancer — which is highly curable when caught early. So any new lump should be checked promptly.

How to check & what to look for

  • Check monthly, ideally after a warm shower when the skin is relaxed
  • Roll each testicle gently between fingers and thumb, feeling for lumps
  • Know what is normal for you (the epididymis at the back is a normal soft tube)
  • Look for a hard, painless lump, swelling, or a change in size/weight
  • Any new lump or change → see a doctor

When to see a doctor urgently

  • A hard, painless lump or swelling in a testicle
  • A feeling of heaviness or a dull ache in the scrotum or lower belly
  • Sudden severe testicular pain (possible torsion — emergency, see next)
  • Any change in size, shape or firmness
Most are harmless — but always check. Testicular cancer is very treatable when found early, and a painless lump is the classic sign, so never ignore one. MedDroid gives general information, not a diagnosis.

Frequently asked questions

Are most testicular lumps cancer?

No — many are harmless cysts or swellings. But because a painless lump can be testicular cancer, which is very curable when caught early, every new lump should be checked.

How do I check my testicles?

Monthly after a warm shower, gently roll each testicle between fingers and thumb, feeling for hard lumps or changes, and get to know what is normal for you.

Is testicular cancer curable?

Yes — it is one of the most treatable cancers, especially when found early, which is why self-checks and prompt review of any lump matter.
